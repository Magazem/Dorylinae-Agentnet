# Concept C: See. Hand off. Decide.

## 1. Title and logline

**See. Hand off. Decide.**

The plan's own promise, that a small team's agents "can see each other, hand each other work safely, and end with a
decision record" (`PLAN:7`), is played as three movements. Each opens on one typed verb and is proven by one real
capture. The film ends on the signed Decision as a document lying on the deep field, the thing a team would actually
show each other, and then on one destination, dorylinae.net.

---

## 2. Strategy

### 2.1 Why a three-verb structure suits people who know nothing about Dorylinae

A stranger cannot absorb a category such as "local-first coordination layer" in a minute. Three plain verbs in a fixed
order are easy to hold, and each one is a benefit before it is a feature. The first verb answers "what does this let
my agent do?" in one word, and the proof that follows answers "is it real?".

- **Benefit first, then process.** For radically new products, abstract benefit framing beats attribute lists
  (Lee, Chu and Roy 2024, evidence.md Q4). A step-by-step demonstration beats a result-only one (Ringler et al. 2024).
  Showing the product in use beats showing how it looks (Frontiers 2022). So every movement runs **verb (benefit) →
  real capture (process) → one plain caption (what you just saw)**. The verbs carry the explanation, the captures carry
  the proof, and nothing is a feature list.
- **One rule set, learned once.** Movement I teaches the grammar: the verb lands huge on the void, rises to a running
  header, the two machines lock in below, one real line changes, one caption names it. Movements II and III repeat that
  grammar exactly, so the viewer reads them faster than the first. This is the ManvsMachine "one rule set governs every
  form" principle and the AKQA "each scene repeats one comparison" pattern (craft.md 1.2, 2.7).
- **The header is the map.** The verbs collect in a small running header (`See.` → `See. Hand off.` →
  `See. Hand off. Decide.`), so a viewer who joins late knows where they are and what is left. The three verbs only
  ever appear together as this header, over product footage. They never sit next to the logo, so they never act as a
  tagline (voice.md: "There is no tagline").
- **The rhythm escalates toward the payoff.** Movement I runs 3 bars, II runs 4 bars and III runs 8 bars. Each one
  lasts longer than the one before because the story is building to the Decision. Craft.md 1.2 allows "one long held
  shot ... for the payoff".
- **Modular by design.** Each movement is a self-contained 7 to 19 s unit with its own verb, proof and caption. The
  15 s and 30 s cutdowns evidence.md Implications §1 asks for come from the same structure: the 15 s cut is the hook
  plus "Hand off." plus the CTA, and the 30 s cut is the hook, all three verbs with their proof shots trimmed, and the
  CTA.

### 2.3 Balance: explaining, showing, calling

| Block | Time | Share | Evidence target (Implications §2) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hook | 0.0 to 2.4 s | 4 % | 0 to 4 s, about 7 % |
| What it is, for whom (explanation only) | 2.4 to 7.2 s | 8 % | 4 to 20 s, about 27 % |
| Three verbs: benefit stated and proven at once | 7.2 to 36.0 s | 46 % | 20 to 46 s, about 43 % |
| Trust (verify, edit, fail) | 36.0 to 43.2 s | 12 % | 46 to 52 s, about 10 % |
| The call (beta facts, then boot and CTA) | 43.2 to 62.4 s | 31 % | 52 to 60 s, about 13 % |

The **explaining** share looks low (12 %) only because the explanation does not stop when the demonstration starts.
Each movement opens on its verb and closes on a plain caption, and the running verb header stays up from 8.4 s to
43.2 s. So from 0 to 43.2 s, about two thirds of the film, a plain-language line is on screen over real product. Mayer's research (evidence.md Q6.1) says the picture should demonstrate
while the words add to it, and a silent film can satisfy that only by layering text over picture, not by alternating
blocks.

The **showing** share (46 % plus the 12 % trust block) sits just above target. The product is unknown, and developers
want real output (Mighty & True). Five of the eight proof beats are real terminal lines, and the other three are the
real signed Decision.

The **call** share is deliberately higher than the 13 % target. That time holds two things the brief requires:

- the owner's beta facts (9.6 s, four lines);
- the brand's unchanged boot with an end card held 3.96 s (craft.md 1.12 asks for about 8 to 9 s).

The call is not left to the end: the tag signals it from 21.6 s, which the mid-roll data favours.

## 3. The film, shot by shot

### 3.0 Conventions for the whole film

- **Clock.** 100 BPM. 1 beat = 0.6 s = 36 frames at 60 fps. 1 bar = 2.4 s = 144 frames. Positions are written
  `bar:beat`, where `1:1` = 0.0 s and t = (bar − 1) × 2.4 + (beat − 1) × 0.6. A "+½" means half a beat (0.3 s) later.
  **Total: 26 bars = 62.4 s.** There are 15 shots, so the average shot is 4.16 s. Every cut lands on a beat, and every
  verb lands on a bar downbeat.
- **Ease.** The default is the brand ease `cubic-bezier(0.2,0.7,0.2,1)` (written **B-ease**). The sweep curve
  `(0.4,0,0.6,1)` is used only inside the boot. Linear timing is used only for the trail march and the 60 ms letter
  stamp.
- **Durations.** 120 ms for state flips, 240 ms for entrances (a whole line lands with a 6 px rise and a 7-frame fade),
  600 ms for plate locks and camera arrivals. Longer camera moves are 1.2 s or 2.4 s. Exits are always cuts.
- **Lock-in** (craft.md 4.3 A). A plate or plate group arrives from the composition's axis outwards with a 45 ms
  stagger. Each plate settles over 500 ms with B-ease from an offset taken from `motion.ts`: sides ±46 brand px, top
  −18 to −30 brand px, bottom +40 brand px. That is ±92, −36 to −60 and +80 px in 16:9 (zoom 2×), and ±138, −54 to
  −90 and +120 px in 4:5 (zoom 3×). There is no overshoot.
- **The stage.** Two terminal plates, `surface` `#0E0C1F` with an 8 brand px radius, on one deep field (`fieldSvg()`
  seed **A**). maya's machine (you) and theo's machine (your teammate) have a band of plain deep field between them.
  - In 16:9: maya's plate on the left bleeds off the left edge, and theo's plate on the right bleeds off the right edge.
  - In 4:5: maya's plate on top bleeds off both sides, and theo's plate below bleeds off the right edge.
  - The band between the plates is where every trail travels. Nothing else ever crosses it.
- **Terminal type** (craft.md 2.2, DESIGN.md §4).
  - Plex Mono. The prompt (`maya$`, `theo$`, the capture's own notation) is amethyst `#A678E6`. Output is `text-3`
    `#8C85AB`, and the one token to notice is pheromone `#D653E1`.
  - Agent commands land whole: 1 frame, then a 120 ms settle. Output lines follow one every 3 frames.
  - In 16:9, terminal text is 24 px (texture) unless a shot gives another size. Whatever must be read is either
    pushed in by the camera to ≥ 64 px or restated in a caption.
  - In 4:5, terminal text is 32 px (texture) and 36 px for a key line.
  - Lines that are too long are cropped at the plate or frame edge. Nothing is wrapped, except the Decision sheet,
    which is a rendered Markdown document.
- **Running header.** Each verb is typed at hero size, then rises into a header in the top-left of title-safe. The
  current verb is in `text` `#EFE9FB` and finished verbs drop to `text-3`.
  - 16:9: Special Elite 64 px, baseline at y 118, from x 96.
  - 4:5: Special Elite 64 px, baseline at y 176, from x 64. Row 1 in 4:5 is kept for the tag.
- **Captions** carry the story.
  - 16:9: IBM Plex Sans 500, 64 px, `text`, left-aligned at x 96, last baseline at y 990 (inside the 1026 px title-safe
    bottom).
  - 4:5: Plex Sans 500, 54 px, left at x 64, last baseline at y 1196 (128 px bottom margin). At most 2 lines.
  - Each caption enters whole with a 240 ms entrance, so it has "landed" 0.24 s after it starts.
- **Specimen labels** (craft.md 4.3 C).
  - Label stock `#F6F3FB`, ink `#17122B`, an inset hairline, radius 2 brand px. Special Elite, typed at 85 ms per
    letter unless a shot gives another speed.
  - A 21° leader line, 2 px, in ink, runs to the thing the label names. Collector dates take the form `08.x.2026` (the
    capture date, 2026-10-08).
  - 16:9: Special Elite 40 px. 4:5: 48 px.
- **The tag.** From 21.6 s to 43.2 s, at the top right in 16:9 (right-aligned to x 1824, baseline y 112) and in row 1 in
  4:5 (x 64, baseline y 100). It reads `Closed beta / dorylinae.net`, in Plex Mono 400 32 px, `text-2` `#A79FC4`. The
  slash is drawn as a 2 px stroke at 21° off vertical, not as a glyph.
- **Colour.** At most one accent per frame (DESIGN.md §2). The accent is either the trail or the one pheromone token.
  Status colours always come with their shape: online is a solid teal `#5CE6C8` dot, offline is a hollow `text-3`
  ring, and an error is `sting` `#F0506E` with a 4 px bar. Label stock appears only for the labels and the Decision
  sheet.
- **Depth.** The deep field moves in parallax during camera moves: far dust 0.2×, stars 0.5×, plates 1×. Plates the
  camera is not focused on drop to `surface` and blur 6 px. Text that is being read is never blurred.
- **Sound palette** (one set, composed in code, key of D, 100 BPM).
  - **Pulse:** a soft felt thump on every bar downbeat. It is the presence pulse made audible.
  - **Bed:** a low sustained pad on D.
  - **Verb motif:** felt-piano notes, See = D4, Hand off = F#4, Decide = A4. The three verbs spell a rising D-major
    triad, and the boot resolves it.
  - **Trail:** one muted wood tick per beat while a trail is in flight, panned from sender to receiver.
  - **Lock:** one soft click per plate group, not per plate.
  - **Online:** a two-note rising bell, A4 to D5.
  - **Invalid:** a single muted C#2 against the D drone. It is the only dissonance in the film.
  - Each new section's bed starts 6 frames before its picture cut (a J-cut). Verb notes land exactly on their picture
    frame. Mix: −14 LUFS integrated, −1 dBTP. Effects sit 6 to 12 dB under the music peaks. There are no per-letter
    key clicks.

## 7. Risks, and what would make it fail

1. **The verbs read as a slogan.** The most likely way this concept breaks a rule is "See. Hand off. Decide." being
   shared as a tagline. Guards:
   - the triplet exists only as a 64 px running header in `text-3`/`text`, over footage;
   - it is never typeset alone, never beside the lockup, and never on the end card.

   If the owner still reads it as a tagline, keep the three title cards and drop the accumulating header. Each verb
   then lives only in its own movement.
2. **"Decide." misread as "the agents decide for you"** (product-facts §5 #21). Guards: the caption says "argue"; the
   sheet shows the human rule labelled "Your rule"; agreement is shown as the CLI's own outcome. The fallback, if
   review is still uneasy, is to change caption 13 to "Two agents argue. Your rule stays in." (6 words, same timing).
3. **Movement III is dense** (rally, sheet, verify, edit in 19.2 s), so strangers may lose the thread at the sheet.
   Fallback: cut shots 10 and 11 (the edit beat). The film becomes 55.2 s, still inside 55 to 65, and ends Movement
   III on the signed sheet. Decide this from the animatic, not now.
4. **Three key lines miss the two-read target** (copy deck #8, #10 and #19 meet the floor only). If the animatic shows
   them under-read, add one bar to Movement II (shot 6 becomes 10.8 s and both of its captions clear their key holds) and
   half a bar to shot 11. That makes 66.0 s, so at the same time shorten shot 10 by 1.2 s (drop the push-in on
   `valid` and the pull-back after it) to land at 64.8 s.
5. **Phone legibility in 16:9.** Terminal text at 24 to 28 px is texture inline on a phone. The design relies on
   captions for every claim and on push-ins to 64 px for the two proof tokens (`online`, `valid, signed by initiator
   and respondent`). If a tester cannot follow with the sound off at arm's length, the push-ins are what to enlarge,
   not the terminal size.
6. **The light sheet as a luminance jump.** It is about 55 % of the frame and locks in over 600 ms, so it is a
   transition, not a flash (WCAG 2.3.1: one event, no repetition). Check H.264 banding at the sheet and sky boundary
   (craft.md 6.2).
7. **The rendered Decision might be read as an app screen.** It has no chrome, no window and no controls; it is paper
   with the file's own text. If QA objects, render it as the raw Markdown in Plex Mono on the same sheet. It loses
   some legibility but nothing else changes.
8. **Honesty edges QA must approve:**
   - **Status swap.** The presence flip swaps two consecutive real `status` outputs in place, which is a re-run, not
     a live update.
   - **Accept timing.** The 25 s between the request (13:40:08) and the accept (13:40:33) is compressed without a
     label (the rally's compression is labelled by its timestamps). If needed, add `13:40:33` as small print under
     theo's lines.
   - **Tamper crop.** It shows one of the two edited spans in `tampered.json`. Showing both costs 0.6 s.
   - **"Signed: maya".** It could read as the person signing rather than her machine; the fallback label is
     "Signed: maya's machine".
   - **Visible test strings.** `demo-review-1` and `unknown` (HUMAN column) are visible as texture. Both are honest. The
     first can be cropped if it distracts.
9. **It feels like slides.** Three cuts to void with a big word could read as a deck. What prevents it:
   - each title lasts only 1.2 s;
   - the verb is typed on the downbeat with its note;
   - the word physically travels into the header while the stage locks in beneath it, so the transition is a
     typographic move inside one rhythm.
