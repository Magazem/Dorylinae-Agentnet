# Concept A: Seven observations

## 1. Title and logline

**Seven observations.**
A naturalist's notebook, typed one entry at a time, watches a piece of work stop travelling by copy and paste
and start travelling from one coding agent to another, through a relay that sees who, when and how big, but not what.

Runtime **62.4 s** (26 bars at 100 BPM). Nine shots: seven numbered observations, one invitation, one end card.

---

## 2. Strategy

### Why a notebook works for people who have never heard of Dorylinae

A stranger needs three answers, in this order: what is wrong today, what this thing is, and why to believe it.
evidence.md (Q4.2) puts it the same way: "lead with the plain-language 'what it is', then prove it with a concrete,
in-use, step-by-step demonstration". A field notebook delivers that structure naturally:

- **One entry, one sentence, one idea.** Each shot is a numbered observation (`Obs. 1` to `Obs. 7`). It holds one
  typed sentence and the real output that proves it. This is craft.md 1.2 ("one sentence carries one scene")
  turned into the film's structure rather than a style. The numbering shows the viewer how far along they are, and a
  viewer who scrolls in at Obs. 4 still gets a complete claim with its proof.
- **The voice is the brand's own.** voice.md describes the brand voice as "Field notes": short declarative sentences,
  exact nouns, numerals. The film needs no invented tone and no tagline. It reads like the product's documentation.
- **Silent-first by construction.** evidence.md Q2 reports that 69 to 85 % of feed video is watched muted, that Reddit
  measured +8.2 % conversion with overlay text, and that LinkedIn's SRT captions cannot carry brand type. In this concept
  the typed notes are the film. The score adds feeling and never carries a claim.
- **The hook is the pain, in words, from frame 0.** evidence.md Q3 gives the targets: about 1.7 s of attention on mobile,
  at least 2 shots in the first 5 s, brand and subject by second 3 to 5, and the steepest loss between seconds 5 and 7.
  - Frame 0 already shows a complete sentence ("You paste it into a chat.") over the real brief it refers to. It is not
    an empty screen waiting for typing.
  - The first cut is at 3.60 s.
  - The name finishes typing at 4.75 s.
  - The first accent (the pheromone trail) starts moving at 6.00 s, inside the 5 to 7 s danger zone.
  - It avoids the brand-first "Netflix trailer for an API" opening that evidence.md (Implications §6) warns about.
- **Benefit first, then process.** Obs. 2 states the benefit in plain words. Lee, Chu and Roy (2024) find that abstract
  benefit messaging suits radically new products. Obs. 3 to 7 then prove it step by step: request, inbox, answer,
  offline, relay. Ringler et al. (2024) find that process demonstrations beat outcome-only ones, and Frontiers (2022)
  finds that showing use beats showing appearance. Every terminal line is real output from capture/, typeset in the
  brand's terminal style (craft.md 1.8, 2.1, 7).
- **Trust through an honest limit.** evidence.md Q5.3 notes that 46 % of developers distrust AI tools and that they
  reward transparency about limits. The film may not say "secure", "private" or "zero-knowledge" (BRIEF;
  product-facts §5). The signature shot (Obs. 7) therefore shows the relay's own log line and labels what it can see
  (who, when, how big). Showing that limit is what makes "Not what." believable.
- **Leave things out.** evidence.md (Implications §2) recommends three capabilities at most, and craft.md §0 at most 4
  product ideas. This film has three: the hand-off (Obs. 2 to 5), offline delivery (Obs. 6), and a sealed relay
  (Obs. 7). Approvals, grants, debates and Decisions belong in the 90 to 120 s walkthrough.

### Balance: explaining, showing, calling

Shots are counted by the job that leads them:

- **Explain-led, 35 %** (S1, S2, S8 = 21.6 s). Here the sentence is the content and the product material only illustrates it.
- **Show-led, 46 %** (S3 to S7 = 28.8 s). Here real output is the evidence, and the note points at it.
- **Call-led, 19 %** (S9 = 12.0 s). In addition, a quiet persistent tag reading `Closed beta · dorylinae.net` is on
  screen from 16.80 s to 50.40 s.

This matches the evidence.md split of about 35 / 45 / 20. The reasons:

- The product is radically new, so the explanation cannot be skipped.
- Developers believe output, not adjectives, so most of the running time is real terminal text.
- The call can be short because S8 does the self-selection: it says who the beta is for and what it offers. evidence.md
  §5.3 says to "say who it is for in one line" so that the wrong people self-select out.
- The tag gives the mid-video call. Mid-roll calls convert at 22.1 % against 16.9 % for post-roll (Wistia), and the tag
  appears at 27 % of the runtime, close to Wistia's "first quarter" advice for sub-minute video.
- Brand mentions: "Dorylinae:" at 4.75 s, the tag from 16.80 s, the typed wordmark in the boot at 52.9 s and the call at
  57.8 s. That is four mentions. The B2B Institute measured 48 % correct attribution with 3 or more mentions against 32 %
  with one. The lockup also fills the last 3 s, which Reddit's data favours.

### How it uses craft.md

- **Signature moves (4.3).**
  - Move C, the determination label, is the film's voice: every observation is numbered on a specimen card, and the
    relay specimen is labelled like a museum object.
  - Move B, the pheromone trail, is the only thing that travels between places. It stops at the relay and resumes.
  - Move A, lock-in, builds every plate, label and card from the brand's boot grammar. The closing boot is therefore
    recognised as the source of a grammar the viewer has watched all along.
- **Typing grammar (2.2).**
  - Agent commands land whole and output streams in.
  - The observer's notes type at 40 ms per letter.
  - Names, labels, the final "Not what." and the call type at the boot's 85 ms per letter.
- **Other rules followed.**
  - Hard cuts on the beat, with no dissolves (1.10).
  - At most one accent per frame, and none before the product appears (1.7).
  - End card per 1.12.
  - Sizes and safe areas per 5.1 to 5.3, holds per 5.4, honesty rules per 7.
  - None of the clichés in §3.

## 3. The film, shot by shot

### 3.0 Global specification (applies to every shot unless the shot says otherwise)

**Clock.** 100 BPM. 1 beat = 0.6 s = 36 frames at 60 fps. 1 bar = 2.4 s. Positions are written `bar:beat`, 1-indexed:
`1:1` is 0.00 s and `6:1` is 12.00 s. Off-beat events carry a decimal beat; for example `6:1.4` is 0.24 s after beat 1
of bar 6. Every cut lands on a beat. Every shot change from S3 to S9 lands on a downbeat. The S1 to S2 cut is on
beat 3 of bar 2, and the final cut to ground inside S9 is on beat 4 of bar 26.

**Type.**
- **Hero note** (the observation): Special Elite, colour `text` `#EFE9FB`, left-aligned.
  - 16:9: 64 px (display-m at 2×), line pitch 72, x = 96, first baseline y = 250, at most 36 characters per line
    (1212 px, 70 % of the title-safe width).
  - 4:5: 60 px, pitch 68, x = 64, first baseline y = 260, at most 30 characters per line.
  - Line breaks are given per shot.
- **Typing.** Each letter fades from 0 to 1 over 60 ms, linear (the boot's letter fade). There is no caret and no typing
  sound.
  - Hero lines: 40 ms per letter.
  - "Dorylinae:", annotation labels, "Not what." and the call: 85 ms per letter.
- **Observation label** (`Obs. n`): a specimen card built like `specimenLabel()`.
  - Label stock `#F6F3FB`, ink `#17122B`, inset hairline at 55 % ink, radius 2 × zoom.
  - Special Elite 28 px in 16:9 / 42 px in 4:5. Padding is 0.9 × size horizontally and 0.7 × size vertically.
  - Top-left corner at (96, 96) in 16:9 / (64, 64) in 4:5.
  - It locks in with the shot's plates (500 ms, from −36 px in 16:9 / −54 px in 4:5 on y). It is not typed.
- **Corner tag.** `Closed beta · dorylinae.net`, Special Elite, colour `text-3` `#8C85AB`, no card.
  - 16:9: 32 px, right-aligned to x = 1824, baseline y = 128.
  - 4:5: 42 px, left-aligned at x = 64, baseline y = 1190.
  - On screen from 16.80 s to 50.40 s. It enters with a 240 ms opacity fade and leaves on the 50.40 cut.

**Terminal plates.**
- Plate: `surface` `#0E0C1F`, radius 16 (16:9) / 24 (4:5), no border, no shadow. Padding 48 px (16:9) / 72 px
  vertical (4:5).
- Text: IBM Plex Mono 28 px, line pitch 44, in both formats. First baseline = plate top + 78 (16:9) / + 102 (4:5).
- Colours: prompt `maya$` / `theo$` in `brand` `#A678E6`; command text `text-2` `#A79FC4`; output `text-3` `#8C85AB`.
  The one token to notice is set in `text` `#EFE9FB` with a pheromone underline.
- 4:5 layout: plates bleed off both side edges and the text column starts at x = 64. That gives 56 columns inside the
  safe area and about 60 to the frame edge. Longer lines are cropped at the frame edge, except where a shot names a
  terminal width.

**Pheromone** `#D653E1`. At most one pheromone element per frame. It is either:
- the trail between places: 6 px dots, 24 px pitch in 16:9 / 9 px, 36 px in 4:5; or
- a token underline: 6 px dots, 24 px pitch, 10 px under the baseline, in both formats.

There is no pheromone in S1 (the world before the product), S8 or S9.

**Deep field** (`fieldSvg`).
- Seeds: S2 21; S3, S5 and S6 use 12 (maya's sky); S4 29 (theo's sky); S7 3 (nebula 0, density 5, stars only); S9 7
  (the brand default).
- S1 and S8 are flat void `#07060F`.
- The nebula mass is kept out of the text column. Choose the seed placement on a contact sheet and never set text on
  the rose.
- During camera pushes, the far dust layer scales at 0.2× and the star layer at 0.5× of the plate scale change.

**Motion tokens.** The ease is always `cubic-bezier(0.2,0.7,0.2,1)`. Exits are cuts, and nothing dissolves.

## 5. The signature moment

**"Not what." (35.40 to 38.40 s).** The relay's own log line sits pinned under the deep field like a specimen. Three
typed determination labels stand on 21° leaders: `who` over `from=QZ_vPMFN to=ZhbF0Hsy`, `when` over the timestamp, and

completely still. The trail waits, dimmed, at the waypoint. The pulse is gone and only a single low note sounds. Three
seconds later the relay logs `delivered from queue`, the pulse returns on the downbeat, and the trail moves on.

Why people will remember and share it:
- It is a still frame a developer can screenshot and argue about. It is honest: it shows what the relay does see, which
  no other launch film volunteers.
- It is exact: real log, real bytes, real timestamps.
- It could only be Dorylinae: specimen labels, a pheromone trail that stops and starts again, and field-notes understatement.
- It turns the one thing the film may not claim (security) into something it can show: an observation, not a promise.

## 7. Risks, and what would make it fail

1. **Monotony.** Nine typed sentences over plates can feel like a slideshow.
   - Mitigation: each observation changes its device. S1 is a duplicate plate, S2 a two-machine diagram, S4 an inbox
     re-sort, S5 a reverse trail, S6 a trail leaving, S7 a specimen and stillness.
   - The sound motif climbs one note per observation.
   - The film moves from monochrome to colour when the product appears.
   - If the animatic still drags, cut S5's camera push and tighten S8 to 3.5 bars.
2. **The hook reads as abstract.** "it" depends on the viewer reading the brief plate as the thing being pasted.
   - Test it on 5 people who have never seen the product.
   - Fallback copy (same timing): "You paste the context into a chat." (7 words; its hold of 3.6 s still clears the
     2.95 s floor).
3. **"Dorylinae:" with a colon** may read as a heading rather than a sentence. Fallback: "With Dorylinae," / "your
   agent hands it to theirs." Line 1 types at 85 ms per letter from 3.90 to 5.18, and line 2 at 40 ms from 5.20 to 6.40.
   That leaves a 5.6 s hold against the 5.4 s key-claim need for 8 words, so the shot length does not change. The brand
   name finishes at 5.18 s instead of 4.75 s.
4. **Small evidence text on phones.** Terminal text at 28 px and the relay log at 24 px are about 5 to 6 pt when 16:9
   plays inline on a phone. The film still works if every hero sentence (64 px, about 13 pt) is read and no terminal text
   is. Do not let a shot depend on reading the terminal. If S7's labels prove too small in the phone test, push the log
   line in to 1.3× and let its ends bleed off.
5. **"Not what." misread as a security guarantee.** It must always share the frame with "who, when and how big". Never
   cut the line alone into a 15 s or 6 s cutdown.
6. **Two different requests.** The hero review (Obs. 3 to 5) and the flaky-test task (Obs. 6 to 7) are separate real
   captures. The new observation number and the visible new title make the change of subject honest. Never imply that
   the review was the request that waited offline.
7. **Diagrammatic motion that the CLI never printed.**
   - The inbox re-sort (S4) passes through a transient order before landing on the captured order.
   - The 4:5 terminal widths soft-wrap long lines.
   - Both keep characters identical, but the owner or fact-checker should approve them. Fallback for S4: the rows
     stream in already sorted and only the underline marks `high 3000`.
8. **4:5 S7 lacks the `delivered from queue` line** (no clean wrap). Delivery rests on the trail resuming. If the
   fact-checker wants the line, show it as a no-wrap strip cropped at the frame edge.
9. **S8 as a "paragraph".** Five Special Elite lines may be flagged under the brand rule. Fallback: keep lines 1 to 3 in
   Special Elite and move "Free during the beta. macOS, Linux and Windows." to Plex Sans 32 px `text-2` under the call card.
10. **The persistent tag** may feel like clutter or be taken for a slogan. It is a destination in `text-3` with no card.
    If it competes in review, show it only from 16.80 to 31.20 (to the midpoint) and again on the end card.
11. **"Up to 7 days" depends on configuration.** It is true of the built product and the relay's default
    (`--queue-ttl 168h`). If the hosted relay ships with a different queue time, change the line before publishing.
12. **Visible capture artefacts.** `demo-review-1` and `demo-flaky-1` are visible in the commands; omitting continuation
    lines would change the command. maya, theo and atlas are throwaway demo names, and the relay key prefixes are from
    throwaway identities. All are real but meaningless outside the capture.
13. **S9 runs 12 s,** over craft.md's 9.6 s maximum shot length. The boot (fixed 3.6 s), the 1.2 s lockup hold, the
    typed URL and evidence.md's 4 s end-card hold account for it. If the end feels long, set the URL whole (240 ms) and
    end at 61.2 s.
14. **The call depends on a page that exists.** "Apply at" promises an application on dorylinae.net on the day of
    publishing (beta-facts §5). Without it the film breaks its own promise.
