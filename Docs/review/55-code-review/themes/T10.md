## Summary

Verdict: most per-key, per-prefix and per-peer bounds hold. Every long-lived map I found on the
relay and in the daemon is either pruned or keyed by something the attacker cannot mint freely
(paired peers, owned teams, approvals). The chunk reviews already found the sharpest relay
defects (C01-01, C01-02, C03-01) and the local Argon2 blow-up (C06-01). The cross-chunk pass
adds three Mediums, two of them confirmed by tests:
- **T10-01:** a hostile relay, with no pairing, can write about 60 audit rows a minute forever
  (`mail.reject` plus `session.reject`). That is about 29 MB a day into an append-only,
  hash-chained table that nothing can prune.
- **T10-02:** the relay re-sends a key's whole offline queue on every reconnect until it is acked.
  A stranger uploads a queue once and downloads it up to 20 times a minute for 7 days. Measured:
  7 MiB uploaded, 291 MiB delivered in 2 minutes.
- **T10-03:** the daemon has no per-peer bound on stored low- or normal-urgency requests. A paired
  peer can fill the owner's disk at tens of GB a day through the hosted relay's per-key limits,
  and without limit through a self-hosted relay.

The Lows cover these:
- CPU amplification: a keychain call per forged frame (a process spawn on macOS), and an Agent
  Card with no size bound that every session frame re-decodes;
- session and mail queues bounded by count, not bytes;
- an IPC `Accept` error that ends the whole daemon.

The daemon side is where the chunk reviews under-rated growth: nothing in the daemon prunes
`audit_events`, `mail_inbox`, `requests` or held `grants`. The relay, in contrast, has explicit
caps everywhere except the ones listed.

## Questions (theme method)

The theme asks for a growth table (below, "Inventory"), attention to CPU amplification, and the
daemon side.
1. **Growth.** Every resource, its actor, bound and eviction are in the Inventory table. The
   unbounded daemon rows are `audit_events` (T10-01), `requests`/`mail_inbox` (T10-03, O-058,
   O-093), held `grants` (C09-03) and `decisions`/`debates`/`experience_records` (O-171). The
   relay's unbounded rows are `bind_requests` (C03-01) and the redelivered bytes of T10-02.
2. **CPU amplification (one cheap frame → expensive work).**
   - Keychain per forged mail or presence frame: T10-04 (`internal/mailbox/mailbox.go:263-282`).
   - Full `peers` scan plus JSON decode of every card per session frame: C08-07, made worse by
     T10-05.
   - HPKE open and Ed25519 verify per frame: cheap (µs), fine.
   - Argon2id: C06-01 (local IPC) and O-034 (relay, ≤ 4 per pairing).
   - Git subprocesses per fetch op: ≤ 2 in flight per peer (`internal/capability/fetch.go:31`),
     plus O-153.
   - Full derivation per redelivery of a poisoned close: C24-01.
   - PowerShell toast per notification: serial, ≤ 16 queued (`internal/notify/trigger.go:105-117`).
   - Relay: Ed25519 verify per auth (bounded by the upgrade rate); two JSON decodes per frame
     (O-010, C01-04).
3. **Goroutines per message.** None are unbounded per frame. The ones started per event are:
   - `go psender.SendNow` per relay `ready` (`internal/daemon/daemon.go:768`), which queues on
     `sendMu` (see T10-04);
   - `go presenceSender.SyncVisibility` per team change (`daemon.go:377`), bounded by roster mail
     rate;
   - `notify.Trigger.Fire` (≤ 16, `trigger.go:106`);
   - pairing KDF goroutines (C06-01).

   The relay starts `kick`/`flushThenKick` at most once per connection.

## Findings

### T10-01 · Medium · confirmed-test
- **Where:** `internal/mail/open.go:98-101` (every reject reported), `internal/mail/audit.go:47-74` (30/min global, then `Append`), `internal/session/session.go:893-919` (`reject`, 30/min), `internal/session/session.go:533-541` (unpaired frames go straight to `reject`); `internal/store/store.go:22-33` (`audit_events` append-only triggers); `Docs/protocol/audit.md:294` (nothing prunes the log)
- **What goes wrong:** a relay can make the daemon append 30 `mail.reject` and 30 `session.reject` rows a minute, forever, with frames from keys that are not paired. The append-only triggers and the hash chain mean these rows can never be deleted short of discarding the whole audit log. Measured: 335 bytes per row, which is 28.9 MB/day and 10.5 GB/year of owner disk, chosen by the relay.
- **Scenario:** a hostile or compromised relay (hosted, or any relay the user points at) injects one `mail` frame and one `session.data` frame per second to the victim's key. The frames come from random `from` keys and carry garbage payloads. Each frame fails step 1 (`unpaired`) and is audited until the per-minute budget is spent. After a year the user's `dorylinae.db` is over 10 GB, `agentnet log` pages through millions of junk rows, `log --verify` rehashes all of them, and the genuine rejects of real peers are crowded out of the 30/min budget (C07 Q1). The same budget also costs one fsync'd write transaction per row on the daemon's single connection. Test: `internal/audit/zz_review55_T10-01_test.go` → `rows=3000 bytes=1003520 (335 B/row); at 60 rows/min: 28.9 MB/day, 10.5 GB/year, never pruned`.
- **Spec:** rubric §1 relay row ("grow its memory/disk without bound"). audit.md:294: "Pruning the log would break the chain; nothing prunes it in Phase 3 … deferred until a beta user's log gets large". That decision assumed growth from the user's own activity, not from an untrusted relay (reopens the no-prune decision for relay-driven rows).
- **Fix direction:** do not audit rejects of frames from unpaired keys (count them in the log's suppressed line instead), or aggregate rejects into one summary row per window.
- **Related:** escalates C07-02 (it noted "disk growth" for `mail.reject` only, rated Low for the chosen text); C08-05 and O-051 (the log-line side); O-171 (retention).

### T10-02 · Medium · confirmed-test
- **Where:** `internal/relay/relay.go:651-660` (every new connection drains from `cursor` 0), `internal/relay/relay.go:885-921` (`drainStep`), `internal/relay/queue.go:333-355` (`next`: rows stay until `ack`), `internal/relay/limits.go:35` (20 reconnects/min/key)
- **What goes wrong:** queued frames stay in the queue until the recipient acks them, and every new connection of that key re-reads and re-sends the whole queue from the start. Nothing limits how many bytes a key receives, or how often one row is re-sent. A key that never acks therefore turns a one-time upload into a download of up to 20 × its queue every minute until the 7-day TTL. Each re-send is also a SQLite read of up to 1 MiB batches on the relay's single database connection.
- **Scenario:** a stranger (the hosted relay runs without `--accounts`) uses one /24:
  1. Four sender keys queue 32 MiB (the per-recipient cap) to each of 12 of its own offline
     recipient keys. That is 384 MiB, uploaded in about 6 minutes within the prefix byte limit
     and under the hosted `--queue-max-total 1GiB`.
  2. Each recipient key connects, reads everything, never acks, and reconnects 20 times a minute.

  The relay then sends 12 × 32 MiB × 20 ≈ 7.5 GiB a minute (about 1 Gbit/s), which saturates the
  VM's uplink. The attacker needs only matching download capacity. At about 11 TB a day, this
  also runs through Hetzner's included traffic within two days. The drain reads compete with
  `add`/`ack`/`sweep` for the single connection, so queue operations slow down for every user
  (not measured). The frames are the attacker's own, so no one else's data is exposed.

  Test: `internal/relay/zz_review55_T10-02_test.go` → `uploaded 7 MiB once; downloaded 291 MiB in 2 (fake) minutes over 40 reconnects; still queued: 8`.
- **Spec:** `relay-hosted.md` §2. Its limits bound only bytes **sent** ("Bytes sent (non-ephemeral) 32 MiB / min") and memory. No limit covers bytes delivered or redelivered. Rubric §1: a stranger must not take the hosted relay down for everyone.
- **Fix direction:** add a per-key and per-prefix delivered-bytes budget that counts redeliveries, or do not re-send a row delivered to the same key within the last N minutes (or more than K times) until an ack arrives.
- **Related:** O-015 (queue flooding, accepted, is step 1 here), O-028 (ack exempt from rate), C01-01/C01-02 (other hosted-relay availability), C02-02 (sweep). With accounts on, only bound keys of invited accounts can queue, which caps this at 4 keys per account (still about 2.5 GiB/min per account).

### T10-03 · Medium · confirmed-read
- **Where:** `internal/mail/receiver.go:221-225` (`mail_inbox.signed` = full signed plaintext of every applied mail, never pruned), `internal/request/receive.go:270-291` (only `high`/`blocking` are budgeted), `internal/request/request.go:12-16` (request 64 KiB, question 320 KiB); no `DELETE FROM requests` or `DELETE FROM mail_inbox` in production code
- **What goes wrong:** every request a paired peer sends is stored twice: its canonical body in `requests.body` and its signed plaintext in `mail_inbox.signed`. Both copies are kept forever, and a `mail.in` audit row is added as well. The urgency budget counts only `high`/`blocking`, and there is no per-peer cap on stored `low`/`normal` requests, so the only bound is the relay's per-key send rate.
- **Scenario:** a paired peer (modified, buggy, or its agent in a loop) sends `question` requests with 320 KiB of context at `normal` urgency. Through the hosted relay, the per-key limit (32 MiB/min, 120 envelopes/min) allows about 100 a minute, which the victim stores as about 60 MiB/min, or roughly 85 GB a day. Through a loopback or self-hosted relay there is no rate at all. Each request also raises a desktop notification. The owner's disk fills, SQLite then fails every write (mail, audit, approvals), and the rows cannot be removed with any CLI command. The effect is the same with 64 KiB `task` bodies at about 21 GB a day. Auto-declined requests from `trust=relay` peers are stored too (O-093).
- **Spec:** rubric §2 gives exactly this as a Medium example ("A paired peer grows `mail_inbox` without bound"); rubric §1 paired-peer row ("make us store unbounded data"); request.md "they keep `requests.body`, `mail_inbox` … small" (size per row only, no count).
- **Fix direction:** cap stored incoming requests per peer (pending and total per day), prune or blank `mail_inbox.signed` after apply (work-session.md:307 says nothing reads it back), and give the user a prune command.
- **Related:** escalates O-058 (R10 L6, recorded Low for `mail_inbox` only). New evidence: the second copy in `requests`, the rate the hosted limits still allow, and the absence of any per-peer cap for low and normal urgency. Also O-093 and C19-01 (the list views break at 1 MiB long before the disk fills).

### T10-04 · Low · confirmed-read
- **Where:** `internal/mailbox/mailbox.go:263-282` (`MailboxKey`: keystore `Load` per call, under `k.mu`), `:119-124` (`auto` mode tries the keychain first), `internal/keystore/keychain.go:40-78` (go-keyring, 5 s timeout, leaked goroutine on hang); go-keyring v0.2.8 `keyring_darwin.go:43-50` execs `/usr/bin/security` per `Get`; callers `internal/mail/open.go:158-174` (step 3, before any signature), `internal/daemon/daemon.go:746-755` (presence opened **synchronously on the relay read loop**); `internal/daemon/daemon.go:768` + `internal/presence/sender.go:458-470` (identity key loaded per recipient, before checking the connection)
- **What goes wrong:** anyone who can put a frame in front of the daemon and knows its live key_id can make it do a keychain lookup per frame. That means the relay, which sees the 8-byte key_id in clear at `payload[1:9]` of every mail and presence frame. On macOS each lookup is a fork/exec of `/usr/bin/security`; on Linux it is a D-Bus round trip; on Windows it is a `CredRead`. For presence, the lookup runs on the relay read loop, so a hung Secret Service stalls all inbound traffic for 5 s per frame and leaks one goroutine per call.
- **Scenario:** a hostile relay forges `presence` or `mail` frames with `from` set to a paired peer (step 1 passes) and the victim's live key_id (step 3 loads the key), then garbage (step 4 fails). At a few hundred frames a second, a macOS victim spawns a few hundred `security` processes a second. That pins a core, drains the battery, and delays genuine mail behind the serial receiver (O-057 drops). The same relay can also loop `ready`-then-close (C04-02): every cycle runs `go psender.SendNow`, which does one identity-key keychain load per visible peer even while disconnected. Those goroutines queue on `sendMu` without coalescing and can pile up when one pass takes longer than the ~0.5 s reconnect interval (suspected, not measured).
- **Spec:** rubric §1 relay row; theme T10 ("keystore/keychain calls" as CPU amplification). mail.md orders steps 1–3 before the signature, so the lookup before authentication is by design, but not its per-frame cost.
- **Fix direction:** cache the live mailbox private keys (and the identity key) in memory for the process lifetime, as the relay client's signer already reloads them only on use. At a minimum, cache for a few seconds and skip `SendNow` when one is already pending.
- **Related:** C07 lead L3, O-055 (relay replays cost two keychain loads each), O-083, O-084, C04-02, C08-07.

### T10-05 · Low · confirmed-test
- **Where:** `internal/agentcard/agentcard.go:85-101` (checks each skill's text, no count or size limit), `:172` (`Verify`, no size limit), `internal/team/kinds.go:349-356` (roster member cards verified without a size cap), `internal/peers/store.go:373-392` (`Introduce` stores skills and card), `internal/peers/store.go:236-270` (`List` decodes every row's skills), `internal/daemon/ping.go:57-66` (`IsPaired` = `List` per inbound session frame)
- **What goes wrong:** the daemon accepts an Agent Card of any size, limited only by the carrying frame (the relay's 16 KiB `CheckCard` applies only to pairing frames on an honest relay). A team owner can introduce a member whose card holds about 1,700 skills (about 480 KiB). After that, every `peers.Store.List` decodes it: one call per inbound `session.*` frame from any key, one per notification (O-108), and one per `status`/`peers` call.
- **Scenario:** a paired peer that owns a team the user joined puts a sybil member with a 480 KiB card in its roster. From then on, every session frame costs the victim about 2.3 ms of JSON decoding instead of microseconds. A relay flooding junk `session.*` frames from random keys, which are rejected only after the scan, then keeps the session worker busy at about 430 frames/s. The session inbox fills, and genuine ping and fetch frames are dropped. Test: `internal/peers/zz_review55_T10-05_test.go` → `card 492148 bytes accepted by Verify and stored; peers.List takes 2.33ms per call`.
- **Spec:** agent-card.md has no size limit; relay-hosted/pairing `MaxCardBytes` = 16 KiB (`internal/envelope/pairing.go:13`) applies only at the relay.
- **Fix direction:** enforce the pairing limit (16 KiB and a skills-count cap) in `agentcard.Verify`, and replace the per-frame `List` with a single-row lookup (C08-07).
- **Related:** C08-07 (per-frame full scan), O-084, O-108, C20 Q2.

### T10-06 · Low · confirmed-read
- **Where:** `internal/session/session.go:79,220,308-318` (inbox of 256 envelopes, queued before any check), `internal/daemon/mail.go:28,167,187-192` (mail queue of 256), `internal/envelope/envelope.go:15-16,96-104` (frames ≤ 1 MiB; `Parse` keeps the decoded payload)
- **What goes wrong:** both inbound queues are bounded by envelope count, not bytes, and `session.*` envelopes are queued at any size up to the 1 MiB frame. A legitimate session payload is a Noise message of at most 65535 bytes. A relay can therefore pin about 256 × 768 KiB ≈ 190 MiB in the session inbox, plus as much again in the mail queue, while the workers are slow.
- **Scenario:** a hostile relay sends 1 MiB `session.data` frames from random keys while the session worker is slow. The worker can be slowed by T10-05's scan, by a blocked send (it holds `sendMu` for up to 16 × 5 s, C08 Q2), or by keychain waits. About 380 MiB of payload then stays live in the daemon until the queues drain, and the relay can repeat this at will. The amount is bounded, which is why this is Low. On the owner's small laptop it still matters.
- **Spec:** rubric §1 relay row ("grow its memory"); session.md framing (Noise message limit).
- **Fix direction:** drop `session.*` envelopes whose payload exceeds the Noise maximum plus the sid before queueing, and bound both queues in bytes.
- **Related:** O-057 (drop on full), O-083 (presence size cap), C08 Q1.

### T10-07 · Low · confirmed-read
- **Where:** `internal/ipc/ipc.go:115-122` (any `Accept` error other than close returns from `Serve`), `internal/daemon/daemon.go:672` (`return srv.Serve(ctx, ln)` ends `Run`); `internal/service/systemd.go:62`, `launchd.go:75`, `schtasks.go:92` (restart)
- **What goes wrong:** a temporary `Accept` error such as `EMFILE` stops the IPC server, and `daemon.Run` then returns, so the whole daemon shuts down (and the service restarts it). A resource spike that would make one IPC call fail instead restarts the daemon. That loses in-flight fetches and sessions, and after a restart pending approvals are treated as expired.
- **Scenario:** (a) A local agent opens IPC connections until the process runs out of file descriptors. Each connection is held for up to the 30 s idle deadline, and nothing caps concurrent connections (C16 Q1). The daemon exits on the next `Accept`. (b) A paired peer's normal-rate requests fire webhook deliveries that each leak one idle connection (C27-01) until the receiver closes it. Against a receiver that never closes, the fd count grows until `Accept` fails and the daemon restarts. This repeats while the peer keeps sending.
- **Spec:** none; rubric §2 Low (hardening; turns a bounded failure into a restart).
- **Fix direction:** treat `net.Error` temporary and `EMFILE`/`ENFILE` errors as retryable with a short backoff (as `net/http.Server` does), and cap concurrent IPC connections.
- **Related:** C27-01, C16 Q1 (no connection cap, "noted only"), C16-leads-R5 item 3 (this is its verification).

## Checked and fine

- No goroutine is started per inbound frame on the relay or in the daemon (inventory above; `rg 'go func|go \w+\('`).
- `ParseStrict` nesting depth is capped at 10000 by `encoding/json` (probe run, see Commands).
- Request tombstones are capped per peer and pruned at 31 d (`internal/request/cancel.go:295-305`).
- Relay `queue.senders` and `prefixKeys` entries are deleted when they reach zero (`queue.go:173-182`, `relay.go:549-555`).
- The relay's `touchKey` is a conditional `UPDATE` that writes at most once per key per day (`accounts_store.go:450-455`).
- The session `pings` map is evicted by the sweeper (`session.go:872-873`), and `maxPings` = 1024.

## Leads for other chunks

- `internal/presence/sender.go:458-470`: `sendOne` loads the identity key and runs `Team.List` before it checks that a relay connection exists. The per-tick cost with many peers is also a T2 concern (`SendNow` goroutines queue on `sendMu`).
- `internal/presence/sender.go:590-629`: `MaybeResync` runs `Team.Get` for every team id (≤ 32) in every heartbeat, on the relay read loop (T2).
- `internal/relay/relay.go:789,821`: an Info line per routed and queued envelope; a stranger can use journald's rate limit to suppress `event=limit`/`auth_failed` lines (T5, ops).
- `internal/keystore/keychain.go:69-78`: `withTimeout` leaks the goroutine of a hung keychain call; each later call leaks another (T2).

## Notes

- A paired peer can keep one PowerShell (Windows) or `osascript` (macOS) process running continuously and flood the notification centre, because nothing limits notifications per peer; `Trigger` serialises them and drops past 16. This is bounded, so it is listed only in the table.
- The early relay's `LimitNOFILE=8192` with `--max-conns 2000` leaves headroom. Caddy in front absorbs the pre-HTTP phase (C01 lead on TLS floods is Caddy's).
