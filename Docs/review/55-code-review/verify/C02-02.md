# Verify C02-02
Verifier: CR-V2 · model claude-opus-5-5 · elapsed 5 min · commit 6cc26a7
**Outcome:** confirmed (Medium)

**Claim restated:** `queue.sweep` deletes every expired row in one autocommit `DELETE … RETURNING` under `q.mu`, on the single DB connection, with a 10 s context. With `secure_delete=ON` and no `journal_size_limit`, a large batch (a) holds `q.mu` and the connection for the whole delete, or exceeds 10 s, rolls back and retries every minute with a batch that never shrinks, and (b) grows the WAL to about the batch size and leaves it there. Precondition: about 1 GiB of queued frames (the early relay's `--queue-max-total`) enqueued within one sweep interval (1 min), so that it all expires in one sweep 7 days later; or a batch that big after an outage.

**Path traced:**
- `relay.Open` → `go s.sweepLoop(opts.SweepInterval)` (`internal/relay/relay.go:355-356`); ticker → `Server.Sweep` (`relay.go:376-390`) → `q.sweep()` (`internal/relay/queue.go:381-402`).
- `sweep`: `ctx` 10 s (`queue.go:382`, `queueOpTimeout` `:25`), `q.mu.Lock()` (`:384`), one statement `DELETE FROM queue WHERE enqueued < ? RETURNING from_key, LENGTH(frame)` (`:386`). No `LIMIT`, no loop. On error `Sweep` only logs (`relay.go:385-386`), and the next tick retries the same statement.
- The DSN (`queue.go:210`) sets WAL, `synchronous(FULL)`, `secure_delete(1)` and no `journal_size_limit` (default -1); `SetMaxOpenConns(1)` (`:217`).
- Who waits: `add` (`:264-268`) and `ack` (`:360-364`) block on `q.mu`. `add` starts its own 10 s context **before** `Lock()`, so an add queued behind a long sweep has little or no time left once it runs. `next`/`count`/`stats` do not take `mu`, but they need the single connection, so drains stall too.
- Totals: the rows stay counted in `q.total` until the sweep deletes them (`queue.go:82-86`). If the sweep rolls back, `add`'s relay-wide check (`:287-288`) keeps refusing with `queue_full`.

**Checks that could block it:**
- *Relay-wide cap* (`queue.go:287`, deploy `--queue-max-total 1GiB`, `deploy/early/agentnet-relay.service:29`): limits the batch to 1 GiB of frames on the early relay. Code default and self-hosted: 4 GiB (`queue.go:35`). It limits the batch but does not block the stall.
- *Per-recipient/pair/sender caps* (`queue.go:290-309`): per key only. Keys are free without accounts (O-015, relay-hosted.md §2 "Without accounts the per-pair cap does not close M2"), so they do not bound the total.
- *Per-prefix byte rate* (`internal/relay/limits.go:28`, 64 MiB/min per /24 or /48, `relay.go:562-574`): to put 1 GiB into one minute takes about 16 distinct prefixes, which is "many resources" but not hard. Filling the cap slowly (for example one prefix over about 16 min) spreads the expiry over many sweeps of about 64 MiB each, which is harmless. So the bad case needs the fill to be concentrated in time, or the relay to be down or stalled.
- *Accounts* (`--accounts`): would block anonymous senders. Off on the early relay (`agentnet-relay.service:7`, D40).
- *Free-disk floor* (`queue.go:190-202, 284`, deploy 512 MiB): checked only on `add`, so it does not account for the WAL a sweep writes. The runbook says the DB is on the root disk, which is "much larger than 3 GB" (`Docs/ops/early-relay-deploy.md:157-158`). That makes SQLITE_FULL unlikely on the early relay, but the "fits a 3 GB volume" budget there (`:147-150`) does not include a WAL as big as the queue.
- *The context deadline*: it is not a hard bound. The 1 GiB runs below took 10.9 s and 14.4 s and still committed, so the interrupt only fires during the delete phase. Either way the lock is held longer than 10 s.
- *Spec*: relay-hosted.md §2 promises "acks and deletes still work" under low disk, and §3 fixes `secure_delete=ON`. Neither sets a sweep batch size or a WAL limit, and no owner decision accepts this. It is not a duplicate: O-015 is the queue *filling*, and O-027 is the free-disk check failing open on a `Statfs` error. This finding is the delayed sweep stall and rollback livelock after a fill, plus the WAL high-water.

**Test:** `internal/relay/zz_review55_C02-02_test.go`, `TestZZReview55C0202SweepLargeBatch`. It uses the production DSN via `openQueue` and inserts expired rows spread over many keys (`ZZ_MB`, `ZZ_FRAME` set the fill). Command: `go test ./internal/relay -run TestZZReview55C0202 -count=1 -v` (local NVMe, Windows).
- 256 MiB × 1 MiB frames: sweep ok in 2.9 s, WAL 0 → 257 MiB, still 257 MiB after a passive checkpoint, `journal_size_limit=-1`.
- 1 GiB × 1 MiB (early-relay cap): sweep ok but took **10.9 s**. It held `mu` and the connection the whole time. WAL **1031 MiB**, which stays.
- 1 GiB × 16 KiB (65 536 rows): ok in **14.4 s**, WAL **1069 MiB**.
- 2 GiB × 1 MiB (reachable under the 4 GiB default): **`context deadline exceeded` after 10.3 s, rolled back.** 2048 rows left and totals unchanged, so every later sweep repeats this and the relay-wide cap stays held (livelock until the operator steps in).

**Severity reasoning:** Rubric Medium: "Bounded DoS that needs a paired peer or many resources". The actor is an internet stranger (accounts off). They need about 16 prefixes sending within one minute, then a 7-day wait. On the early relay (1 GiB cap, local disk) the measured effect is one stall of about 11–14 s for all queue traffic, and a WAL as big as the queue that persists on disk. On a relay at the 4 GiB default, or on a slower disk, the sweep rolls back every minute: stalls of 10 s per minute and `queue_full` for everyone until an operator steps in. That is close to High ("take the hosted relay down … lock for everyone"). It stays Medium because the hosted relay's configuration keeps the batch at the edge where it still commits, and the attack needs many prefixes and precise timing, so it is not "cheap". The reviewer's rating holds; the confidence is now `confirmed-test`.
