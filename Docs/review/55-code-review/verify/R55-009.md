# Verify R55-009 (T10-02) — relay queue redelivery bandwidth amplification
Verifier: R55-V009-Opus · model claude-opus-4-8 · elapsed ~35 min · commit `eadf189` (current `main`, F1 merged)
**Outcome: CONFIRMED — reproduces on current main; F1 did not change it.**
**Severity recommendation: High** (borderline; the consolidator kept Medium as D2). Reasoning below.

## Claim restated

Queued frames stay in the relay's queue until the recipient acks them, and every new
connection of a key re-reads and re-sends that key's whole queue **from cursor 0**. Nothing
bounds bytes *delivered* or *redelivered* per key or per prefix. A stranger (accounts off on
4.1p) queues frames to its own offline keys once, then has each key connect, read everything,
never ack, and reconnect up to 20×/min for the 7-day TTL. The relay re-sends the whole queue
on every reconnect: a one-time upload becomes an unbounded download (relay egress + single-DB
read amplification). Precondition: free keys, one or a few /24s (or /48s), and download
capacity to match the relay's egress.

## Did F1 change it?

**No.** F1 (R55-F1, `Docs/review/56-r55-f1-spec.md`, merged) added a per-frame read deadline,
three memory budgets (outbound / read / ephemeral) with per-prefix 1/8 shares, and
heaviest-then-stale eviction. None of that is a *delivered-bytes rate cap*:

- The drain path is unchanged in nature. `serve` → `startDrain`/`drainStep`
  (`internal/relay/relay.go:709-720, 990-1053`) still reads `s.q.next(c.key, c.cursor, …)`
  from `c.cursor`, and `newConn` (`internal/relay/conn.go:80-83`) leaves `cursor` at 0, so
  **each reconnect re-drains from the start**.
- Rows survive until ack: `queue.next` (`internal/relay/queue.go:335-357`) selects
  `seq > after AND enqueued >= cutoff` and only `queue.ack` (`:360`) deletes. Never acking
  keeps every row for the full 7-day TTL.
- The reconnect limit is still 20/min/key (`internal/relay/limits.go:34`,
  `defaultReconnectsPerMin = 20`).
- The only budget on the drain is the **outbound memory budget** (`c.reserve`/`drainReserve`,
  charged `kindOutbound` in `conn.go:239`). It bounds *concurrent in-flight memory*, not
  throughput: it is released as the recipient reads, so a recipient that keeps reading cycles
  through its share repeatedly with no rate limit.
- F1's per-minute **byte** limits (`KeyBytesPerMinute` 32 MiB, `PrefixBytesPerMinute` 64 MiB,
  `limits.go:31-32,27`) and the R55-035 "charge bytes at read" change are charged on the
  **read/upload** side (`led.charge(c, kindRead, …)`, `conn.go:302`) — the sender's uploaded
  bytes, not delivered bytes. Downloads are not rate-limited.
- Eviction never fires here: the attacker's recipient reads its whole queue fast and closes,
  so it is never the *stale* heaviest holder (`evictStale` = `2 s + held ÷ 512 KiB/s`,
  review 56a A1). Eviction protects memory, not egress.

R55-F2 (report §4: findings 009/010/011) is the ticket that would add a delivered-bytes budget.
It is **not merged**.

## Measurements and realistic cost (4.1p on Hetzner)

Extrapolating the confirmed single-key ratio to the report's scenario (12 offline keys ×
32 MiB = 384 MiB queued, 20 reconnects/min/key): 12 × 32 MiB × 20 ≈ **7.5 GiB/min ≈ ~1 Gbit/s
≈ ~11 TB/day** of relay egress. My 4.1p test measured 145 MiB/min for one 5.5 MiB key, which
scales to the same order (32/5.5 × 12 × 145 MiB ≈ 10 GiB/min).

- **Attacker upload:** one-time ~0.4 GB to fill the queues (~6 min under the 64 MiB/min prefix
  byte limit; within the 1 GiB `--queue-max-total`). Negligible.
- **Attacker download:** to *realise* the egress the attacker must read at line rate (TCP
  backpressure means relay egress = attacker read speed). ~1 Gbit/s of download is one
  commodity cloud VM — Hetzner itself provisions ~1 Gbit/s+. This is the whole cost, and it is
  **cheap and one host, not "many resources."**
- **VM uplink:** the deployed unit is the *smallest shared* Hetzner type
  (`early-relay-deploy.md` §1 step 3). ~1 Gbit/s of sustained egress saturates its shared
  uplink, so honest mail delivery, acks and presence stall for everyone during the attack — a
  restart does not help while the attacker reconnects.
- **Traffic allowance / cost DoS:** Hetzner Cloud includes ~20 TB/month egress (then ≈ €1/TB).
  At ~11 TB/day the month's allowance is gone in **under two days**, after which the owner pays
  overage or the traffic is throttled — a pure-cost attack on top of the availability hit.
- **Single-DB load:** every redelivery is a `queue.next` SELECT of up to 1 MiB batches on the
  relay's single SQLite connection (`queue.go:335`, `drainBatchBytes`), competing with
  `add`/`ack`/`sweep` (all serialized on `q.mu` / the one connection). Queue latency degrades
  for all users; not separately measured here.

## Severity reasoning (rubric `01-rubric.md` §2)

- **Not Critical:** no content, key, identity or code-execution impact. The frames are the
  attacker's own; nothing is disclosed.
- **High criterion met:** "a cheap unauthenticated attacker can take the hosted relay down
  (OOM, disk, fd, lock) for everyone." Actor = internet stranger, free keys, accounts off on
  4.1p. The upload is one-time and trivial; the sustained cost is ~1 Gbit/s of download from a
  single commodity host. The effect reaches **everyone**: uplink saturation starves honest mail
  delivery, the traffic allowance is exhausted in ~2 days (cost DoS), and the single DB
  connection is loaded. It applies to 4.1p exactly as configured (confirmed by test).
- **Why the consolidator kept Medium (D2):** the Medium row is "bounded DoS that needs a paired
  peer or **many resources**"; the stated caveat is that the attacker needs "matching download
  bandwidth." My verification shows that download requirement is **one gigabit host**, which is
  not "many resources" under the rubric — it is squarely the cheap-stranger case the High
  bandwidth clause names. The one genuine difference from R55-001/002 (which hold the budget
  with *zero* ongoing bandwidth) is that R55-009 is **bandwidth-priced**: impact lasts only
  while the attacker sustains the download. That makes it the *weakest* of the hosted-relay
  Highs, but still High.

**Recommendation: raise R55-009 to High** and settle D2 accordingly. If the owner weighs the
"must sustain egress" requirement heavily, Medium is defensible, but the amplification factor
(one-time ~0.4 GB upload → ~11 TB/day egress) tips it to High in my read.

## Fix-direction notes for the F2 spec writer

The report/theme fix direction (a per-key/per-prefix delivered-bytes budget counting
redeliveries, or no re-send of a row to the same key within N min / more than K times without
an ack) is correct. Implementation-specific notes from reading current main:

1. **The root is `c.cursor` resetting to 0 per connection** (`conn.go:80-83`,
   `relay.go:1035`). A persisted per-key delivery cursor (highest seq delivered, survives
   reconnects) would stop re-sending already-delivered-but-unacked rows on the next connect —
   but be careful: a genuinely dropped frame must still be redeliverable, so pair it with a
   per-row redelivery counter or a "not before" timestamp on the queue row rather than a hard
   skip. The theme's "no re-send of a row to the same key within N minutes / > K times without
   an ack" is the safer framing.
2. **Add a delivered-bytes token bucket** alongside the existing per-key/per-prefix *upload*
   byte buckets in `limits.go` (which today only charge `kindRead`). Charge it in `drainStep`
   before `sendReserved`. This is the cheapest bound and mirrors the existing rate machinery.
3. **F1 interaction:** do not rely on the outbound memory budget or eviction — both are
   memory/instantaneous, and a keeping-up recipient is never evicted (measured). F2 needs a
   *rate/volume* limit, orthogonal to F1's memory fairness.
4. **DB angle:** a delivered-bytes cap also bounds `queue.next` re-reads on the single
   connection, which addresses the un-measured DB-contention half of the finding.
5. **Overlap with R55-010:** a non-base64 / unparseable payload is never acked either
   (client returns before ack), so it rides the same redelivery loop for 7 days. F2 should
   cover both: bound redelivery *and* let the relay/client drop frames that can never be acked.
