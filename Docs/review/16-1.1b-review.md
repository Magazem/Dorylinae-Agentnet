## Checked, no finding

- **Forged or non-owner roster.** Rule 2 requires `team.owner = msg.from`, and `msg.from`
  is authenticated by the mail layer. A known team with a different stored owner is
  ignored (`not_owner`). A member cannot introduce anyone: only rosters that pass the owner
  checks reach `Introduce`.
- **Owner impersonation via trust `team`.** An introduced peer that claims ownership of a
  roster is ignored with `owner_trust`, because only `code` and `fingerprint` pass.
- **Replay and rollback.** Epochs at or below the stored one are ignored silently, and
  `mail_seen` dedupes exact replays. A left, removed or dissolved team re-enters only by
  consuming a pending join.
- **Pending-join matching.** Rows are keyed by `owner_key = msg.from`, so a third party
  cannot satisfy someone else's pending join.
- **Forged `team.leave`.** The only key it can remove is `msg.from`. A non-member, the owner
  itself, or a team that is not owned and active is ignored.
- **Cap and DoS.** Rosters are limited to 1–32 unique keys. Every card and announcement is
  verified. Overall size is bounded by the mail limits.
- **Order in one transaction.** Teams and members are upserted, then peers are introduced,
  then `GCIntroduced` runs, all in the Apply transaction (the review 14 rule). GC fails
  outbox rows through `removeTx`.
- **`ErrBadBody` classification.** Shape, card and announcement failures (except the time
  window) return `ErrBadBody`. Trust, lookup, epoch and state ignores return `nil`. Database
  errors are retryable (the review 13 rule).
- **What a non-owner learns.** Only what team.md specifies. The removal roster is
  owner-only.
- **Concurrency.** `SetMaxOpenConns(1)` serialises roster, leave and `peers_remove`, and all
  Apply work uses `tx`.
- **Migration 9.** It runs as one `Exec` inside `apply`'s transaction, so all four tables
  are created or none are.

## Medium (fixed)

**M1. A pending join could be consumed by a roster the joiner should not accept.**
`kinds.go` consumed the pending-join row before it checked `selfIn`, and it never checked
the wire `state = active` that team.md rule 4 requires. Two consequences:

- A dissolved roster that listed self, for an unknown team or for one in local state
  `left`/`removed`/`dissolved`, was applied as `dissolved`. Its members were introduced and
  then GC'd, and the invite was used up.
- A roster that did not list self was ignored but still deleted the row.

Fix: consume only when `selfIn && wireState == active`. Tests:
`TestRosterDissolvedDoesNotConsumePendingJoin`, `TestRosterWithoutSelfDoesNotConsumePendingJoin`.

**M2. A `team.join` from a current member failed Apply forever.**
If a key that is still on the owner's roster sends `team.join` (for example, it was
re-invited after leaving locally but before its `team.leave` arrived), the
`INSERT INTO team_members` hits the primary key. The error does not wrap `ErrBadBody`, so
the receiver never acks. The sender then resends for 14 days, and the invite is never
marked used.

Fix: when the key is already a member, `applyJoin` marks the invite used and only bumps the
epoch (new `bumpEpochTx`, now shared by add and remove). The following broadcast is newer
than the joiner's copy, so its pending join admits the roster. `applyJoin` also refuses an
invite whose team is not owned by self, instead of letting `ErrNotOwner` become a retry
loop. Test: `TestJoinFromCurrentMemberBumpsEpoch`.

**M3. `peers_remove` could leave the owner's teams active with no retry path.**
`trust.go` deleted the peer row first and then called `OwnerRemoved` in a separate
transaction. If `OwnerRemoved` failed, the teams stayed `active` and their introductions
stayed trusted. A retry returned `unknown_peer`. This is exactly the state team.md
§Removing the owner forbids.

Fix: `OwnerRemoved` runs first, then `Remove`. A failure now leaves the peer in place, so a
retry works. `ErrNoPeer` is tolerated only when GC itself removed that row.

**M4. `Broadcast` was all-or-nothing and not snapshot-consistent.**
It aborted on the first failed `Submit`, and on any member without a `peers` row: the
owner ran `peers remove` on a member of its own team. After that, every later roster for
the team (join, leave, rename) reached nobody. Team and member reads were also separate
statements, and the body was rebuilt once per recipient (32× card parsing).

Fix: `rosterSnapshot` builds the full and owner-only bodies once, in one transaction, and
closes it before any `Submit`. The database has one connection, and `Announcement()` may
write, so it is called before the transaction opens. A member without a peer row is left
out of the body and the recipients, with a warning. `Submit` errors are collected with
`errors.Join`. Test: `TestBroadcastSkipsMemberWithoutPeerRow`.

## Low (fixed)

- **L1.** A new peer could get a `keys` push after being inserted and GC'd in the same
  roster transaction (a roster that removes self but lists a new key). Such peers are now
  filtered out of `newPeers`. No keys leaked: `Submit` to a key that is not a peer fails.
  Test: `TestNoKeysPushToPeerCollectedInSameRoster`.
- **L2.** `team.epoch < 2^53` was not enforced. Fixed. Test: `TestRosterEpochAt2Pow53IsBadBody`.
- **L3.** `harness_test.go` used `os.MkdirTemp` instead of `internal/testutil.TempDir(t)`.
  Fixed.

## Low (open)

- **L4.** A missing owner row at the trust lookup returns a retryable error. This only
  happens when racing `peers_remove`, and the sender's resend is then rejected at mail
  step 1. Harmless.
- **L5.** The process-global `pendingRoster`, `pendingJoin` and `pendingLeave` maps keep an
  entry if the commit fails after Apply stored it. The leak is tiny.
- **L6.** The owner operations do not check state. `Leave` accepts a team that is not
  active, and `Rename`, `Delete` and `AddMember` accept a dissolved team. 1.1c should gate
  these (`team_*` IPC).
- **L7.** `RemoveMember` does not audit `team.member_remove`. The 1.1c caller must.
- **L8.** Per team.md Apply step 3, a dissolved roster or a self-removed roster introduces
  unknown members, which GC then deletes. This writes `peer.remove` audits for peers that
  were never usable. Spec option: introduce only when the local state becomes `active`.
- **L9.** Team-id squatting. A member M of P's team knows P's team id. If a victim accepts
  M's invite and receives M's roster carrying that id first, the victim ignores P's later
  rosters as `not_owner`. This needs the victim's consent to M's invite (OD-P1-2 spirit).
  Note it in team.md.
- **L10.** Pending joins match on the owner only, not on the lookup or the team, so any
  active roster from that owner consumes one. This is spec-intended (the owner is trusted).
  It is recorded here for 1.1d.
- **L11.** The roster broadcast after commit is not outboxed atomically with the epoch
  change. A crash between them relies on the presence-epoch resync (1.2).
- **L12.** Spec gap: team.md does not say what the owner's `peers remove` of a member of an
  owned team does. After M4 the member is left out of rosters but stays in
  `team_members`. Recommendation: remove it and bump the epoch, decided in 1.1c or the
  spec.
