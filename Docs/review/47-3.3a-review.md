## What holds

- **Domain separation.** The Decision message is `"dorylinae-decision-v1\n" ‖ canonical`. Every
  other Ed25519 message signed with the identity key has its own prefix, ending in `\n`:
  mail `dorylinae-mail-v1\n`, mailbox key `dorylinae-mailbox-key-v1\n`, card
  `dorylinae-agent-card-v1\n`, grant `dorylinae-grant-v1\n`, relay auth
  `dorylinae-relay-auth-v1\n` (the nonce comes after the prefix), and noise static
  `dorylinae-noise-static-v1\n`. None of these is a prefix of another, so a Decision signature
  cannot be reused as a mail, grant, card or relay-auth signature, and the reverse is also
  impossible. The audit chain and the approval codes are hashes or HMACs, not signatures. The
  id domain (`dorylinae-decision-id-v1\n`) is only ever hashed.
- **What a signature binds.** The canonical Decision contains both identity keys, the session,
  the request, the team and the id. `bodyRow` ties `debate.close` and `debate.sign` to the
  session derived from `(from, to, request)`. A close or a sign cannot be moved to another
  debate, and a sign whose hash differs from the one A stored is a refusal (`hash`).
- **Order of B's checks.** `closeMatches` runs first (review 45 H1 holds; a contradicted close
  is now refused, not ignored). Next come `closeGap` (`entries`, `cut`, `unsent`, or hold),
  then the constraint hold, then B's own derivation (`ErrInconsistent` → `outcome`,
  `ErrClosedBeforeOpened` → `time`), then the hash, then A's signature under `r.peer`. B signs
  only after all of them pass, and only over its **own** derived bytes. The only inputs A
  supplies are `entries`, the constraint list, the outcome/reason pair (checked by rule 6) and
  `at` (checked now, M1). A replayed close for a closed or broken B is stored for the record
  and ignored. A second close while one is held replaces the held body and is checked in full
  again. `TestDecisionRefusals` covers each refusal reason end to end, through A's
  `peer_refused`.
- **Truncation.** Slots are contiguous (`Next`), so `entries` is a prefix. A cannot cut an A
  entry B holds (`cut`), cannot count a B slot B never sent (`unsent`), and cannot keep
  `agreed` once B's answer is cut (`ErrInconsistent`). What remains is the dropped-last-entry
  case that §Security considerations accepts (L6).
- **Single signature never counts as confirmed.** A's row is `awaiting_peer` with
  `sig_respondent` NULL until B's signature verifies over A's stored bytes. B stores A's
  signature only after it has verified it, and on a refusal B stores no signature at all.
  `Verify` sets `Complete` only when both signatures are present and valid. A file signed only
  by the respondent fails step 1. If initiator = respondent, step 5 catches it, so one
  signature cannot count twice.
- **Keys.** Decisions are signed with the identity key (`identityPriv(ks)`, the same seed as
  mail and cards), never the mailbox key. `participants` come from `r.keys(s.Self)`, and B
  verifies A's signature under `r.peer`, which is the `from` of the authenticated mail. A
  rotated identity is a new peer (new sessions, new ids), so an old debate cannot be confused
  with it. L3 adds the self-check that the stored seed matches the card.
- **Constraints.** B's own constraints are stored at approval, before they are sent. A
  constraint received from A is stored with `author = other(role)`, taken from the
  authenticated sender and never from the body. So a listed id can bring into B's Decision only
  (a) B's approved constraints or (b) A's constraints marked `by: initiator`. A cannot put an
  unapproved text into B's record under B's name. A can leave B's constraints out of the list
  (they become `late`), which is the rule-9 authority the spec gives A.
- **Canonical form.** Both sides derive from the same canonical bytes: the request `body`
  (canonical on both sides), the canonical entries and the constraint rows as sent. The
  numbers are integers built with `fmt.Sprint`, the times are whole-second UTC strings taken
  verbatim, optional members are omitted when empty, and there is no Unicode normalisation
  anywhere. The independent canonicaliser in `tools/verifyvectors` reproduces the vector.
  Differences of this kind can only come from a modified daemon, and they end in a refusal,
  never in two different signed records.
- **Size.** `MaxDecision` is checked on the canonical bytes before signing and storing, on
  both sides, and the worst-case test derives about 740 KB. Every embedded member was
  validated on receipt. Verify now enforces the same cap (L2).
- **Independence.** `internal/decision` imports only `internal/agentcard`, and the debate
  validators are injected through `Schema`, so `decision verify` can run without a daemon
  (3.3b). `tools/verifyvectors` imports no `internal/` package. It has its own
  canonicaliser, its own id/hash/signature code and its own step 1–5 checker.
- **Audit.** `decision.create` holds `{id, session, peer, outcome, hash, bytes, signed_by}`,
  `decision.refuse` holds a fixed reason word, `decision.sign_in` holds ids, and the late entries
  are audited as `debate.ignored {reason: "late"}`. None of them carries text, and
  `noContentInAudit` and `TestDecisionHeldForConstraint` check this.
- **Migration 20.** The table matches §Storage. `decisions` is dropped by every rewind list
  (`store_test.go` ×3, `migration19_test.go`, `audit/chain_test.go`), in reverse order.

**The worker's open points.**
- (1) A listed B constraint that B never sent cannot be detected, so B holds the close:
  **accepted, and the spec is corrected (L4).** Such a constraint cannot exist. Any id B lacks
  is either an A constraint still in flight or an id A invented. Neither one can enter B's
  record under B's name, and holding is safe because B signs nothing.
- (2) On a refusal B stores its own derivation, unsigned, as `peer_refused` with
  `peer_hash` = A's claim. When it cannot derive one it stores nothing and sends
  `Hash(empty)`: **accepted, and written into the spec (L5).**
- (3) The refusing B goes to `broken`, cancels its mirror and sends `debate.broken`. A goes to
  `closed`, `peer_refused` and `debate.broken`: **accepted, and written into the spec (L5).**
- (4) B's entries at slots ≥ `entries` are shown as `late` only in the view: **accepted.** The
  rows are left unchanged, the audit records the drop, and the spec now says so.
- (5) IPC exposure is 3.3b's work: **accepted** (see L7 for what 3.3b must handle).

## Medium

### M1: a close earlier than the request's `created` was signed by both sides but fails verify (fixed)

`decision.Derive` copied `closed` from A's close `at` without comparing it with `opened` (the
request's `created`). Verify step 5 requires `opened ≤ closed`. If A's clock went back between
creating the request and closing it (an NTP step, or a VM restored from a snapshot), or if a
modified A chose any earlier `at`, both daemons derived the same bytes, B signed, and both
stored a `signed` Decision. `agentnet decision verify` then rejected the file at step 5. The
result is split: the daemons show a confirmed record that the offline checker, and anyone the
file is given to, rejects.

**Fix.**
- `Derive` returns `ErrClosedBeforeOpened` when `closed < opened`.
- On A, `decideTx` reads the request's `created`. When the close `at` is earlier, it sends
  `created` as `at`, so an honest A with a clock that went back still closes and B signs.
- On B, the error becomes a refusal with reason `time`. Because B cannot derive a record,
  `refuseOnB` stores no row and sends `Hash(empty)`.
- decision.md rule 11 now states this.

*Tests:* `TestDeriveRefusesClosedBeforeOpened`, `TestDecisionClockWentBack` (A's clock goes back
72 h: the close carries `created`, and both sides sign a Decision that verifies), and
`TestDecisionRefusals/closed_before_opened`.

## Low

### L1: signatures and keys were decoded with non-strict base64url (fixed)

`VerifySignature` and verify's key check used `base64.RawURLEncoding`, which ignores the unused
low bits of the last character. Each signature therefore had several text forms that all
verify. This does not break authenticity, but byte comparison of files, deduplication and
"same signature" checks become unreliable. Both now use `RawURLEncoding.Strict()`, and
decision.md step 1 says so. *Test:* `TestVerifyRefusesNonCanonicalBase64`.

### L2: verify did not enforce `MaxDecision` (fixed)

The schema bounds each member but not the number of `problem.context` or `human_decisions`
entries, so a hand-made file with thousands of references passed step 1. Step 1 now refuses
`canonical(decision)` over `MaxDecision`. *Test:* `TestVerifyRefusesOversize`.

### L3: the Decision signer did not check that the stored key matches the card (fixed)

`relayclient.keystoreSigner` refuses a keystore seed that does not match the card, but
`debate.Store.sign` did not. With a mismatched keystore (a restored or replaced key file), A
would send a signature that B refuses as `signature`, and both humans would see a "modified
daemon" refusal. `sign` now refuses, so the close fails locally with a clear error.
*Test:* `TestDecisionKeyMismatchSignsNothing`.

### L4: the spec asked B to refuse a listed B-authored constraint it lacks, which cannot be detected (spec fixed)

§Signing step 2 said a missing "listed **B-authored** constraint id" means a refusal. B cannot
tell whose an unknown id is, and as the constraints bullet above shows, no such id can enter
B's record under B's name. The text now says that a missing listed id is always held, and
explains why that is safe.

### L5: B's refusal record and both sides' phases were unspecified (spec fixed)

§"If B refuses" now says that B stores its own derivation (rule 6 on its own transcript),
**unsigned**, as `peer_refused` with `peer_hash`. When it cannot derive one, B stores no row
and sends `Hash` of the empty canonical form. B goes to `broken`, and A goes to `closed` with
`debate.broken`.

### L6: A can drop B's last entry by claiming a timeout (noted, accepted in the spec)

`closeMatches` accepts `escalated/timeout` whenever B's position exists, and B does not compare
its own sent time with the turn deadline. A modified A can therefore cut B's last move or
answer, provided A sent nothing after it. The result is always `escalated`, never `agreed`.
B's view shows the entry as `late`, and both humans are notified, as §Security
considerations "Truncation by A" states. Checking B's own deadline would narrow this, at the
cost of refusing honest closes when the clocks disagree. No change.

### L7: for 3.3b, B's `peer_refused` row has no signatures (noted)

A refused record on B has `sig_initiator` and `sig_respondent` both NULL (by design, L5).
`decision_show` and the `--json` export must not emit `"initiator": ""`, and the Markdown must
render the record under the refused banner, with no signature section. A's `peer_refused` row
has A's signature only and follows the unconfirmed path.

### L8: a lost `debate.sign` is not re-sent (noted)

When B's `debate.sign` expires in the outbox (A offline for more than 7 days, D10), B is
`signed` and A stays `awaiting_peer` for good. A re-sends its close only as the echo that
follows an ignored B entry, which a closed B no longer sends, and B ignores a close for a
closed debate anyway. The outcome is safe: A only understates
what it has. A later ticket could make B re-send `debate.sign` for its stored signed row when
a close arrives again.
